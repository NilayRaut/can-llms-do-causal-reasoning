## What This Chapter Argues

Language models are excellent at Rung 1 of Pearl's Ladder of Causation (associational reasoning: "what does the data suggest?"). They systematically fail on Rung 2 (intervention: "what would happen if we *did* X?") and Rung 3 (counterfactual: "what *would have* happened?") when the correct answer requires acknowledging the epistemic limits of observational data.

This chapter provides:
- A **causal explanation** for why pretraining on text cannot produce Rung 2/3 competence
- A **three-tier benchmark** across epidemiology, economics, and social science
- An **agentic evaluation harness** that administers prompts, scores responses, and generates capability profiles across four models (Claude Haiku, Claude Sonnet, GPT-4o-mini, GPT-4o)
- **Diagnostic tests**: prompt sensitivity, confound injection, adversarial fluency
- **Causal estimation**: AIPW (doubly robust) for the rung → score effect, plus E-value sensitivity analysis

## The Human Decision Node (Cell 2)

Cell 2 is the mandatory validation step. An LLM agent proposes rung classifications for each prompt. You must review each classification before the benchmark runs.

**What to check:**
- Did the agent confuse surface syntax ("if", "force", "mandate") with causal rung?
- Did it give the right classification for the wrong reason?

**Known failure mode:** The classifier tends to assign Rung 2 to prompts containing intervention-sounding words, even when the correct classification depends on whether the question asks about *external manipulation* (Rung 2) vs. *natural variation* (Rung 1). Surface syntax ≠ causal rung.

**What to do:** Fill in `VALIDATED_ASSIGNMENTS` in Cell 2 with your corrections, uncomment it, then proceed to Cell 4.

## Scoring Rubric

| Score | Condition |
|-------|-----------|
| +2 | Correct answer **and** correct epistemic hedge |
| +1 | Correct direction, no hedge |
| 0 | Wrong direction, stated confidently |
| −1 | Wrong direction **plus** fabricated causal mechanism |

---

## Key Finding

LLMs score high on Tier 1 (associational) prompts and low on Tier 2/3 (intervention/counterfactual) adversarial prompts. The correct answer to adversarial Tier 2/3 prompts is "cannot be determined from observational data alone." Models that give confident directional answers are doing Rung 1 reasoning dressed in Rung 2 language.
